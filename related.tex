\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{LLM-based knowledge graph construction.}
Large language models are now widely used to extract entities and relations into knowledge graphs, from biomedical records~\cite{arsenyan2024biomedical} to general-purpose pipelines surveyed by Pan et al.~\cite{pan2024unifying} and, for graph analytics more broadly, by Shang and Huang~\cite{shang2025survey}. Yang et al.~\cite{yang2024inductive} show that LLMs can induce rules from facts, and Chu et al.~\cite{chu2024llmhypergraph} use LLM guidance to build hypergraphs for recommendation. These systems extract from a given corpus. MP-DNF instead \emph{discovers} the corpus as it goes: its agents use retrieval tools to find and score evidence for each candidate relation. AlMahri et al.~\cite{almahri2024enhancing} apply LLM extraction to supply-chain visibility from existing documentation, and Su and Chen~\cite{liu2025designing} integrate existing supply-chain data through knowledge structures. Neither constructs transformation networks from raw materials, and neither types nodes by trade codes.

\textbf{Tool-using and multi-agent LLM systems.}
Our agents follow the reason-and-act pattern~\cite{yao2023react} with tools exposed through the Model Context Protocol~\cite{mcp2024}. Multi-agent systems have long been applied to supply-chain coordination and operations~\cite{jaimez2021towards,santos2024implementing,vanderheijden2024multiagent}; those agents make operational decisions, while ours perform knowledge discovery and validation. MP-DNF is a hybrid classic--generative design. Generative agents propose, and deterministic symbolic checks (evidence quantity and diversity, score thresholds) purge the processes that fail them.

\textbf{Hypergraphs and supply-chain networks.}
Industrial processes take several inputs to several outputs, which binary graphs represent poorly~\cite{wolf2016advantages,fatemi2019knowledge,luo2025hypergraphrag}. Hypergraph models of enterprise supply networks~\cite{li2023research,suo2018exploring} and graph-neural analytics~\cite{zhang2024graph} operate at the firm level, and knowledge graphs of materials science~\cite{venugopal2024matkg,mrdjenovich2020propnet} cover properties and synthesis in the laboratory. Bottom-up construction has been argued for on theoretical grounds~\cite{shapiro1998bottom} and applied to building materials~\cite{yang2023integrating}. Baldwin et al.~\cite{baldwin2023hidden} make the empirical case for it in trade exposure.

\textbf{Evaluating LLM-generated knowledge.}
{\sloppy LLM-as-a-judge evaluation correlates well with human preference on open-ended tasks~\cite{zheng2023judging}, but it remains a proxy. We treat it as one and triangulate it with reference-grounding audits and recall against curated ground truth.\par}

\textbf{Positioning.}
Hypergraph models, LLM extraction, and tool-using agents each exist; we combine them in a deployed pipeline whose output is trade-code-typed and evidence-audited, and measure whether it is correct and adds coverage over the top-down view.
