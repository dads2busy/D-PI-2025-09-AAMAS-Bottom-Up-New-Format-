\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{LLM-based knowledge graph construction.}
Large language models are now widely used to extract entities and relations into knowledge graphs, from biomedical records~\cite{arsenyan2024biomedical} to general-purpose pipelines surveyed by Pan et al.~\cite{pan2024unifying} and, for graph analytics more broadly, by Shang and Huang~\cite{shang2025survey}. Yang et al.~\cite{yang2024inductive} show that LLMs can induce rules from facts, and Chu et al.~\cite{chu2024llmhypergraph} use LLM guidance to build hypergraphs for recommendation. These systems extract from a given corpus; MP-DNF instead \emph{discovers} the corpus as it goes, using agents with retrieval tools to find and score evidence for each candidate relation. AlMahri et al.~\cite{almahri2024enhancing} apply LLM extraction to supply-chain visibility from existing documentation, and Liu et al.~\cite{liu2025designing} integrate existing supply-chain data through knowledge structures; neither constructs transformation networks from raw materials, and neither types nodes by trade codes.

\textbf{Tool-using and multi-agent LLM systems.}
Our agents follow the reason-and-act pattern~\cite{yao2023react} with tools exposed through the Model Context Protocol~\cite{mcp2024}. Multi-agent systems have long been applied to supply-chain coordination and operations~\cite{jaimez2021towards,farazi2025enhancing,santos2024implementing,vanderheijden2024multiagent}; those agents make operational decisions, whereas ours perform knowledge discovery and validation. The design of MP-DNF is a hybrid classic--generative one: generative agents propose, and deterministic symbolic gates (code validity, evidence quantity and diversity, score thresholds) decide admission.

\textbf{Hypergraphs and supply-chain networks.}
Industrial processes take several inputs to several outputs, which binary graphs represent poorly~\cite{wolf2016advantages,fatemi2019knowledge,luo2025hypergraphrag}. Hypergraph models of enterprise supply networks~\cite{li2023research,suo2018exploring} and graph-neural analytics~\cite{zhang2024graph} operate at the firm level; knowledge graphs of materials science~\cite{venugopal2024matkg,mrdjenovich2020propnet} cover properties and synthesis in the laboratory. Bottom-up construction has been argued for on theoretical grounds~\cite{shapiro1998bottom} and applied to building materials~\cite{yang2023integrating}, and the empirical case for it in trade exposure is made by Baldwin et al.~\cite{baldwin2023hidden}.

\textbf{Evaluating LLM-generated knowledge.}
LLM-as-a-judge evaluation correlates well with human preference on open-ended tasks~\cite{zheng2023judging} but is a proxy; we treat it as such and triangulate it with reference-grounding audits and recall against curated ground truth.

\textbf{Positioning.}
The individual components---hypergraphs, LLM extraction, tool-using agents---are established. The contribution here is their integration into a deployed pipeline whose output is trade-code-typed and evidence-audited, and an evaluation that measures whether that output is correct and whether it adds coverage over the top-down view analysts use today.
