\newcommand{\PaperAbstract}{%
Analysts assessing critical-mineral supply chains work top-down from finished products through supplier records, which hides the intermediate materials and processes where disruptions actually originate. We present MP-DNF, a deployed agentic pipeline that builds a \emph{Material Evolution Knowledge Hypergraph} (MEKH) bottom-up: six role-specialized LLM agents, equipped through the Model Context Protocol with harmonized-trade-code search, web search, and a reference cache, iteratively propose HS-6-typed materials and multi-input/multi-output industrial processes, while a deterministic purge removes processes whose reviewed evidence score or scale score falls below a threshold. The pipeline was developed for, and is in use by, analysts at a university national-security policy research institute.
We evaluate the MEKHs for boron, gallium, germanium, and cobalt without expert annotation: an LLM judge from a different model family than the generator finds nearly all \JudgeNAll{} processes plausible but only \JudgePrecisionAllClaude{} fully correct, with HS-code assignment the dominant error; cited references mention at least one input and one output for \EvidenceFullBoron{} of boron hyperedges; the boron MEKH recovers \RecallLenientBoron{} of curated textbook routes and contains \CoverageMekhOnlyBoron{} HS-6 codes absent from the USGS commodity listing. Coupling an earlier boron MEKH to UN Comtrade data localizes cascading export-disruption risk to a few concentrated intermediate codes.
}
